## Final story

High above Hollowmere Cove, in a cozy cave lined with smooth stone, lived Sorrel. Sorrel was a young dragon with warm copper scales. They glowed like the last light of a campfire. Below her, the village sparkled. Cottages wore soft lanterns in their windows, and each one looked like a little golden moon.

When the sun slipped down, the sky turned deep blue. Sorrel poked her snout out of the cave. "I wish I could watch the stars," she whispered. "I wish I had a new friend to watch them with." Above the quiet green meadow, the first stars blinked on. One by one, they winked at her. Sorrel hoped someone would share the sky with her.

Sorrel felt a small flutter in her tummy. The night was so big and so dark. What if the dark felt lonely? She pulled a thick blanket of soft moss over her head. She curled up small, like a snail in its shell. "Just a little while," she told herself. Under the moss, it smelled of rain and wild thyme. Her heart beat fast, then slowly, slowly, it calmed down.

Then came a cheerful voice. "Sorrel? It's Tilda Moss, from next door!" Tilda climbed the hill, and Grandmother Plum followed behind her, carrying a lantern on a long wooden stick. Together they lit a row of tiny lanterns along the path. Each one glowed like a star resting on the grass. "You are not alone," said Grandmother Plum. "Peek out when you are ready." Tilda sat down beside Sorrel and told a story about a moon that loved to hum. Grandmother Plum told one about a brave little bird. Slowly, Sorrel peeked out from the moss. The stories made her feel warm and safe.

Together they looked up at the twinkling stars. Sorrel counted them until she lost count. Tilda pointed at the brightest one, and Grandmother Plum smiled. Then Grandmother Plum gave a big yawn. Tilda yawned too, and then Sorrel yawned, a long, soft yawn. They tucked in, each one safe at home, as the village grew quiet and the lanterns dimmed one by one. Goodnight, little dragon, sleep tight.
