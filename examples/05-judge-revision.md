# Example 5: the quality judge improving a story

**Request:** My name is Sam and I live on Maple Street. Tell a story about a bunny.

**Run:** `runs/20261102-201142-546770.json`, saved from an earlier version of the prompts.
The planner later stopped inventing names for requests that name characters, and the judge
was told not to ask for personal details. This run predates both fixes, and its judge notes
show the problem: rounds 0 and 1 ask the story to name the child and the street.

This is an authentic run. It is kept as it happened, including the notes that were wrong.

## Rounds

| Round | Review | Judge overall | Judge notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | passed | 7.5 | In the calm_wind_down part, add one line where Tansy waves goodnight to Sam on Maple Street, since the request named them. / In the gentle_want part, add one sparkling detail, such as a dewdrop on a thimbleberry, to give the meadow a moment of wonder. / In the calm_wind_down part, change 'drizzle' to a simpler phrase like 'a soft, sprinkly rain' so young listeners follow it easily. |
| 1 | passed | 7.8 | In the setup part, add one line that names Sam and mentions Maple Street, so the story answers the personal details in the request. / In the small_obstacle part, replace 'did not budge' with 'did not move at all' to match a young reader's vocabulary. / In the calm_wind_down part, explain 'drowsy' in context, such as 'grew quiet and sleepy.' |
| 2 | passed | 8 | In the setup part, add one line that mentions Sam and Maple Street, such as Tansy's burrow being near Sam's home on Maple Street. / In the kind_solution part, add one small delight moment, such as a raindrop sparkling on Odette's whiskers under the leaf. |

Three drafts were judged, and the best scoring draft (round 2, overall 8) was kept.
Round 0 scored 7.5. Each revision applied the judge's notes.

## Round 0 draft (before revision)

Tansy Dewbloom was a young bunny with long, floppy ears. She lived in a snug burrow at the edge of Thistlewhistle Meadow. Her bed was lined with soft green moss. Each morning, sunbeams peeked through the grass. The flowers nodded sleepily in the warm breeze. Tansy loved her cozy home very much.

One sunny day, Tansy hopped along the path beside a tall hedge. It was covered in thimbleberry bushes with red, shiny berries. Tansy wished she had a friend to play with. Then she stopped. Under a leaf sat a little field mouse. Her name was Odette.

Odette sat very still, with her tail curled tight. Tansy's heart went thump, thump, with hope. "I would like to be your friend," she whispered.

Just then, a light rain began. Pit, pat, went the drops on the leaves. Odette wanted to go through the round wooden gate to play. But the gate was stuck shut. She pushed and pushed, but it did not budge. Odette wanted to call out for help. Her voice felt too small, so she stayed quiet.

Tansy took a deep breath. "Odette, are you stuck too?" she called kindly. Odette nodded. "Let's ask for help together," said Tansy. Soon, old Mr. Wendell Fernsby came shuffling down the path. He was a kind hedgehog gardener with a straw hat. "Let us wiggle this gate," he said with a smile.

Mr. Fernsby pushed. Tansy pushed. Odette pushed too, with all her tiny might. Creak! The gate swung open. Then Tansy plucked a broad leaf from the hedge. "Would you share my umbrella leaf?" she asked. Odette gave a shy smile. They tucked under it together, dry and happy.

The rain softened into a gentle drizzle. Mr. Fernsby tipped his hat and wandered home. Odette waved a small paw. "Goodnight, Tansy," she said. Tansy hopped back to her burrow and curled into her mossy bed. Outside, the meadow grew quiet and drowsy. The flowers folded their petals, and the crickets hummed low. Goodnight, little one, sleep tight.

## Final story (round 2, after revision)
