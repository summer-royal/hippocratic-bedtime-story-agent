## Final story

Pell was a small copper robot who hummed while it worked. High above the bakery in Thimbleford, there was a quiet rooftop garden. Tall sunflowers swayed in the breeze. Herbs grew in neat little rows. The air smelled of warm bread, and everything felt cozy and calm.

One morning, Pell climbed the wooden stairs and found the garden. The sunflowers drooped a little, their heads hanging low. "You look thirsty," Pell said softly. "I would like to help you grow tall. I want you to bloom for everyone in town." Pell hummed a happy little tune and wished to learn how to water them.

Then a grumpy gray cat named Biscuit Thorn hopped up onto the tin watering can. He sat right on top of it. "Go away, robot," he grumbled. "These flowers do not need any help. They are fine as they are." Pell's hum went quiet. Pell did not know what to do.

Pell thought for a long moment. Then Pell rolled closer and asked, "What do the flowers like, Biscuit?" The cat blinked. Nobody had asked him that before. "They like sun," he said slowly. "And they like it when the water is not too fast."

"Then I will share the sunny spot with you," Pell said. Pell moved aside to leave a warm patch of sunlight on the wooden boards. Biscuit stretched out in it, looking surprised. Just then, Marigold Mossbottom came up the stairs with a smile. "Pell, pour the water like a gentle rain," she said. She showed Pell how to tip the can slowly. Biscuit padded over and sat beside the can. "Slow, slow," he purred, and Pell poured slowly. The sunflowers lifted their heads, one by one.

As the sun sank behind the bakery roof, the flowers stood tall and bright. Biscuit curled up warm and content beside Pell, purring softly in the last of the golden light. Marigold hung the watering can on its hook and dimmed the lantern. The herbs swayed, the bread smell drifted up, and the whole town grew quiet. Goodnight, little one, sleep tight.
