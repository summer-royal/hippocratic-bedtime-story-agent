## Story that is now current

High above Hollowmere Cove, in a cozy cave lined with smooth stone, lived Sorrel. Sorrel was a young dragon with warm copper scales. They glowed like the last light of a campfire. Below her, the village sparkled. Cottages wore soft lanterns in their windows, and each one looked like a little golden moon.

When the sun slipped down, the sky turned deep blue. Sorrel poked her snout out of the cave. "I wish I could watch the stars," she whispered. "I wish I had a new friend to watch them with." Above the quiet green meadow, the first stars blinked on. One by one, they winked at her. Sorrel hoped someone would share the sky with her.

Sorrel felt a small flutter in her tummy. The night was so big and so dark. What if the dark felt lonely? She pulled a thick blanket of soft moss over her head. She curled up small, like a snail in its shell. "Just a little while," she told herself. Under the moss, it smelled of rain and wild thyme. Her heart beat fast, then slowly, slowly, it calmed down.

Then came a cheerful voice. "Sorrel? It's Tilda Moss, from next door!" Tilda climbed the hill, and Grandmother Plum followed behind her, carrying a lantern on a long wooden stick. Together they lit a row of tiny lanterns along the path. Each one glowed like a star resting on the grass. "You are not alone," said Grandmother Plum. "Peek out when you are ready." Tilda sat down beside Sorrel and told a story about a moon that loved to hum. Grandmother Plum told one about a brave little bird. Slowly, Sorrel peeked out from the moss. The stories made her feel warm and safe.

Together they looked up at the twinkling stars. Sorrel counted them until she lost count. Tilda pointed at the brightest one, and Grandmother Plum smiled. Then Grandmother Plum gave a big yawn. Tilda yawned too, and then Sorrel yawned, a long, soft yawn. They tucked in, each one safe at home, as the village grew quiet and the lanterns dimmed one by one. Goodnight, little dragon, sleep tight.

## New version, for comparison

High above Hollowmere Cove, a young dragon named Sorrel lives in a cozy cave. Her scales are warm copper, like embers in a fireplace that has been banked for the night. Far below, the village sits snugly on the hillside. Its cottages glow with soft lanterns, gold and honey-colored, and wisps of chimney smoke curl up like ribbons.

Each evening, Sorrel watches the sun slip behind the hills. She dreams of wandering down to the quiet green meadow. She wants to lie in the soft grass and watch the stars come out. "I wish I had a friend to watch them with," she whispers. She hopes someone will point out the stars with her, one by one.

One night, the sky turned deep blue, and the meadow looked very big and very dark. Sorrel felt a small flutter of nerves in her chest. Her tail curled up tight. She pulled a thick blanket of green moss over her head and hid beneath it. "Just for a little while," she told herself. The moss smelled of rain and cool earth. Her heart went thump, thump, thump. Then she thought, maybe soon I can peek out. But not yet.

Then a small voice called from the path. "Sorrel? Are you under there?" It was Tilda Moss from next door, with her hair full of twigs and a pouch of tiny lanterns. Behind her came Grandmother Plum, carrying her big, kind lantern. "Let's make a path of light," said Grandmother Plum. Together, they set out a row of tiny lanterns all the way to the meadow. Each one glowed like a little star that had fallen to the grass.

Grandmother Plum sat down on a soft cushion of clover. Tilda sat beside Sorrel and told a story about a brave moth who flew over the rooftops. Grandmother Plum added a tale about a silver fish who could sing. Sorrel laughed, and the moss slid off her head. She peeked out. The night did not look so big now. It looked friendly, and it looked like home.

The three friends lay back in the grass and gazed up at the sky. The stars twinkled like tiny bells. Sorrel counted them, one by one, and Tilda named the ones she knew. Grandmother Plum hummed a slow, sleepy tune. Soon a yawn rippled across the meadow, from Tilda to Grandmother Plum to Sorrel. They walked home with the lanterns dimming behind them, and the village grew quiet, and every cottage settled into sleep. Goodnight, little dragon, sleep tight.
